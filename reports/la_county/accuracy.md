# Accuracy test: permitted buildings vs. this tool

Generated by `python -m zce accuracy`. Do not edit by hand.

For 52 cities, every building of 2+ homes in the state's Annual Progress Reports (first permitted 2018-2025) is matched to its lot and compared with this tool's number for that lot.

**How to read this.** A building larger than our number means we may have the rule wrong, or the project may have used something we don't model, such as a bonus program, a variance, an older rule or a rezoning. This test can only catch numbers that are too low. It **can't show a number is too high**, because most buildings don't use all their allowed capacity. Passing doesn't prove a rule is right.

Other limits: rules and zoning are today's, while permits date back to 2018. A lot may have been merged or split since, and a townhome project can show as one home on each tiny lot. The multifamily ADU row is a likely explanation, not a confirmed one. Homes on one parcel are added across years, and homes kept on the lot aren't counted.
