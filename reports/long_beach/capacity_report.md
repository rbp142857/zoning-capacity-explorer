## Realistic vs. theoretical

Theoretical capacity assumes every lot is rebuilt to the maximum. The realistic estimate counts only lots that pass every redevelopment screen (see METHODOLOGY.md), and only their *local* capacity: adding an ADU or a second house under state law usually keeps the existing home, and single-family zones are left out (see below).

- Net new homes, theoretical (all rules incl. state law): **174,405**
- Net new homes on lots **likely to redevelop**: **1,450** on 135 lots (0.2% of modeled lots)
- Single-family zones (SB 9, ADUs): **158,648** new homes in theory on 56,372 lots. Not counted as likely: small-scale infill on single-family lots has produced few homes wherever it's been allowed, whether through state law or a city reform.

## State law

- **Accessory Dwelling Units and Junior ADUs** (Cal. Gov. Code §§66314-66342 (recodified from §65852.2 by SB 477, effective 2025)): applied on 12,201 lots
- **SB 9 two-unit developments and urban lot splits** (Cal. Gov. Code §65852.21 (two-unit developments) and §66411.7 (urban lot splits)): applied on 55,370 lots
- **AB 2097 no parking minimums near major transit** (Cal. Gov. Code §65863.2): applied on 15,917 lots
- **SB 79 transit-oriented development upzoning (2025)** (Senate Bill 79 (2025-2026 session)): **not modeled** (capacity is underestimated)
- **State Density Bonus Law** (Cal. Gov. Code §65915): **not modeled** (capacity is underestimated)

## Assumptions

- Average new unit: 850 sf inside the unit; 80% of building floor area is inside units, so each unit uses 1,062 sf of building
- Parking: surface parking, 300 sf of ground per stall including aisles
- Every zone-specific assumption is written next to its value in `cities/pasadena/rules/*.yaml`.
