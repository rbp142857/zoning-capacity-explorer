      Theoretical vs. likely to redevelop

      Theoretical numbers count every home the rules allow, as if every lot were rebuilt. Most lots won't be. Likely to redevelop counts only lots that pass every common planning screen: zoning allows at least twice the homes already there, the building is at least 50 years old (or the lot is vacant), the building is assessed at less than the land, and the lot isn't a condo or in a landmark district. Only the city's (or the scenario's) own allowance counts here.

      Lots in single-family zones are not counted, whatever allows more homes there (SB 9, ADUs, or a city duplex or fourplex reform): wherever small-scale infill like this has been allowed, it has added few homes compared with how many lots became eligible. Those homes still appear in the theoretical numbers.

      This is a screen, not a forecast: it shows where rebuilding is plausible, not when or whether it will happen. The building-value screen is weak in California, where Prop 13 makes assessed values depend on when a property last sold.
